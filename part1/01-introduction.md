# Introduction and System Selection

This proposal reviews Credit Xpress, a licensed Malaysian moneylender that offers small personal loans through an Android app, an iOS app and a website. We chose it because it is a local service that Malaysians might turn to when short on cash, yet, unlike global platforms, it receives almost no outside scrutiny.

Credit Xpress meets the three conditions in the brief. It is real and usable hands-on: the app is free to install from Google Play and the App Store and runs without an account up to the sign-up screen, where our first two findings occur. It is also small and local: it operates only in Malaysia, and we found no academic studies of the app itself. Published research covers only the wider category of digital loan apps (Akanji, Egele and Stringhini, 2026; Pervez et al., 2026), so a first-hand review of a licensed local lender adds evidence this literature lacks.

It also has clear ethically relevant features. It collects personal and device data, including location and identity details, which the privacy policy states are used “for risk assessment and scoring” (Credit Xpress, 2026a), an automated decision about people. It handles loans and repayments, and its users are likely to be vulnerable people who need cash quickly. For these users, a design choice that pressures them to share data can end up shaping a decision about their money, which raises the stakes beyond privacy alone.
