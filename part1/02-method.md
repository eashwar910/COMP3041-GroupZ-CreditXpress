# Method

We combined three techniques. First, a hands-on walkthrough: we installed the app fresh on a test device with an empty contact list and recorded every screen from first launch [device: Xiaomi Pad 7, Android version 16, app version 2.1.2, 26/10/2026]. To see how the app handles refusal, we repeated the flow denying each permission request. Second, we read the app's privacy policy to find what each requested data type is used for. Third, we compared what we observed with Android's permission guidance and Google Play's policies for loan apps.

We then carried out a second hands-on walkthrough on iOS, again without creating an account [device: iPhone, iOS version [FILL IN: Settings > General > About], app version [FILL IN: App Store > CreditXpress], 29/10/2026]. On iOS the app opens to a home screen that anyone can browse, so we tapped every element on it to record what a person can learn before signing in. We also read the in-app privacy policy, the terms and conditions on the company website (Credit Xpress, 2026b), and visited the domain the policy names as its data-processing domain. We compared these with Apple's App Review Guidelines for personal-loan apps.

We used only our own details, entered no phone number and did not apply for a loan. We used no surveys or interviews, as these require ethics approval. Our findings reflect two devices (an Android 16 tablet and an iPhone), and app version 2.1.2 on Android and [FILL IN] on iOS.
