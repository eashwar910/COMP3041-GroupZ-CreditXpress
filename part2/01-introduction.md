# Introduction

Credit Xpress is the app brand of Opg Capital Holdings Sdn Bhd, a moneylender licensed by Malaysia's Ministry of Housing and Local Government (KPKT) under licence WL3337/07/01-11/020227. It offers short, unsecured personal loans through iOS and Android apps and a website. In the app, loans run up to RM10,000, repaid in 3 - 6 instalments, at a stated "daily rate" of 0.05% [Figure 5].

The service targets people who need cash quickly and may struggle to borrow from a bank. Applicants need a MyKad and a stable income, but "no payslip or bank-statement upload is required". The website says it is "built for the gig drivers, market sellers, dual-income families", and its first example use is a "Medical Emergency" (Credit Xpress, 2026c). The app is not niche: on 2 November 2026 the iOS version had 953 ratings and ranked No. 30 in Malaysia's Finance chart (OPG Capital Holdings, 2026a).

Credit Xpress shows how app-based lending changes what a credit decision is built from. Instead of payslips, the website's privacy policy describes collecting location "for risk assessment and scoring", the whole address book, 12 months of financial SMS messages, installed apps and calendar entries (Credit Xpress, 2026a). The policy linked from the app stores is narrower: it covers SMS and calendar data, but reads device contacts only "If the app requests access to device contacts in a specific version", with "explicit consent" (Credit Xpress, 2026d). Research links this kind of collection to predatory lending (Pervez et al., 2026), including harassment that affects "both borrowers and their contacts" (Akanji, Egele and Stringhini, 2026). Credit Xpress is licensed and says it keeps to the legal cap of 18% a year, but a licence settles little: Akanji, Egele and Stringhini (2026) found widespread non-compliance among approved loan apps. That makes it a harder case. The question is not whether a scam is unethical, but whether a lawful lender's software treats its users, and the people around them, fairly.

## What is new since Part 1

Part 1 reported four issues: location requested before sign-up (A), repeated permission prompts (B), cost shown only as a daily rate (C), and a lender that cannot be checked or contacted without signing in (D). Re-checking the public channels on 2 November 2026 added three findings:

1. The Android listing we recorded on 26 October returned "not found". A new "CreditXpress" listing by the same developer, updated 30 October, shows "0+" downloads (OPG Capital Holdings, 2026b).
2. The stores and website state an 18% maximum APR, which the in-app home screen did not show on 29 October [Figure 5]. The channels also disagree: tenure is "up to 91 days" in the app, "30 to 91 days" on the website and "Minimum 91 days, Maximum 273 days" in both stores (Credit Xpress, 2026c; OPG Capital Holdings, 2026a, 2026b).
3. Both stores' privacy declarations list Contacts among the data collected, and the Android one adds SMS and installed apps (OPG Capital Holdings, 2026a, 2026b).
