# Stakeholders

We group stakeholders by how close they are to the app: direct users, the operator and its workers, business partners, and people who never use the app but are affected by it. Appendix A summarises each group with the issues and evidence that apply.

## Direct stakeholders

**Prospective borrowers.** These are people who have installed the app but not signed up. On Android they meet Issues A and B before they can do anything else. The approximate-location prompt fires straight after the disclosure statement, and leaving the login page fires it again [Figures 1 - 4]. Android's guidance says that if a user taps Deny more than once, the system treats it as a permanent denial (Android Developers, 2026). A person who refuses both prompts has therefore used up every request the operating system allows, before seeing any loan terms. On iOS, the same person sees "Licensed in Malaysia" but cannot open it without signing in through WhatsApp [Figure 6]. The effect is on their choice, not yet on their money. They are asked to decide about data before they can judge the lender.

**Borrowers.** Once a person applies, the stakes become financial. The app's home screen shows "Daily rate 0.05%" and no APR [Figure 5]. The terms bar cancellation once an application is submitted, and say only that interest is "as stated in the Moneylending Agreement" (Credit Xpress, 2026b). The new evidence makes this harder to excuse as one oversight: the three channels give three different tenures, so a borrower who compares them cannot tell which offer applies. Borrowers' devices also feed the scoring, through the address book, SMS and installed-app data the website's privacy policy describes (Credit Xpress, 2026a). They have little power to refuse. Someone borrowing for a hospital deposit, with no payslip to offer instead, is likely to accept any request that stands between them and the cash.

A sub-group is **people without WhatsApp**. The only sign-in route is a WhatsApp number and OTP [Figure 4]. They cannot apply, and inside the app they also cannot reach support or check the licence.

## The operator and its workers

**Opg Capital Holdings** gains from each feature. Early prompts secure location permission before a user has committed, and the website policy says location is used "for risk assessment and scoring" (Credit Xpress, 2026a). A low headline rate may also help conversion. It also carries the risk. Its KPKT licence runs until 2 February 2027 (Credit Xpress, 2026c), and its store presence is fragile: the Android listing we used on 26 October had been replaced by 2 November (OPG Capital Holdings, 2026b). We do not know why.

**The app's developers** are the people the SE Code addresses. They built the prompt that fires on leaving the login page (Issue B) and the support icon that leads to sign-in (Issue D). Their documents show signs of little review. The iOS policy refers to "Android runtime permissions" [Figure 8]. The website policy says contact data "will not be stored, shared, or transmitted" yet "will be uploaded to CreditXpress's servers" (Credit Xpress, 2026a). Developers may not decide when prompts fire or what the home screen says; product or growth staff may. Section 4 returns to this, because it limits what the Code can ask of one engineer.

**Collection and support staff** apply what the software gives them. The terms say the company or "appointed third parties" will contact borrowers about missed payments (Credit Xpress, 2026b). If contact lists are collected, these workers are the ones who could use them. Malaysia's Consumer Credit Act 2025 now requires debt collection agencies to be licensed (New Straits Times, 2026), which makes any outside collector a regulated party too.

## Business partners

**Google and Apple** host the app and set the rules it is judged against. Google bans personal-loan apps from reading contacts (Google, 2026), yet the Play listing shows Contacts as a collected data type (OPG Capital Holdings, 2026b). From outside, we cannot tell whether the app requests the banned permission or gathers contacts another way, such as the emergency-contact form [Figure 9]. Apple requires loan apps to disclose the maximum APR (Apple, 2026); the store listing does, the home screen did not. **Meta** is a partner by default, as WhatsApp is the only sign-in channel. **Credit reporting agencies, banks and e-wallets** receive borrower data or move funds (Credit Xpress, 2026b).

## Indirect stakeholders

**Emergency contacts and address-book contacts** are the clearest indirect stakeholders. They never install the app, and neither policy describes any way to ask for their consent. The in-app privacy policy lets the company collect emergency-contact names and numbers [Figure 9]. The website version goes further and claims the whole address book (Credit Xpress, 2026a). Both store declarations list Contacts as collected (OPG Capital Holdings, 2026a, 2026b). Research links this data to collectors pressuring a borrower's friends and family (Akanji, Egele and Stringhini, 2026). We found no evidence that Credit Xpress does this, and did not test it. What we can show is that the data comes from people with no relationship to the lender and no stated way to object.

**Borrowers' households** share the result of a loan. The website's own examples, a hospital deposit and car repairs, are household costs (Credit Xpress, 2026c). If the price was unclear (Issue C), the family carries it.

**KPKT and the licensed-lending market** are affected at one remove. The 18% cap is the main legal limit on price (The Vibes, 2025), and a daily rate makes it harder for borrowers to check. Licensed lenders also depend on looking different from illegal loan apps. A licensed app that collects contacts and puts its licence behind a login inside the app blurs that line.

## Fair reading

Some of these effects have legitimate reasons. A Malaysia-only lender needs to screen out overseas applicants, and approximate location is not banned for loan apps (Google, 2026). Credit-report checks are a normal and lawful step. An emergency contact can help a lender reach a borrower who has changed number. The concern is that the people who bear the costs are not the ones who choose. Prospective borrowers decide before they have information, and contacts are not asked at all.
